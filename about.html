Product Design Career

A Decade Designing Software for Engagement

Ethan James Caldwell spent roughly a decade as a product designer, working on consumer software built to capture and hold attention. Now based in Austin, Texas, the Northlight founder treats that period as the foundation of his current work rather than a chapter set aside. The design choices that drive engagement were familiar to him because he had helped make them.

Inside that industry, success had a precise definition: time-on-app, session depth, and return frequency. Each figure rewarded products that kept people scrolling, tapping, and coming back. Caldwell came to see those numbers as measures of extraction rather than value, and concluded that optimizing for engagement meant working against the user. His career history is summarized on his LinkedIn profile, and the turning point is examined in the piece on the Northlight founder on attention and design.

Northlight

Northlight, Founded in 2021 on a Counter-Thesis

In 2021, Caldwell founded Northlight with an explicit counter-thesis to mainstream consumer software. Where most products are evaluated by how much time they absorb, Northlight was built to be judged by how much focus it returns to its users. Every feature is weighed against that standard: does it increase focus, or does it simply increase time on the platform?

The approach did not limit growth. Northlight began as a focused tool and has grown into a community of hundreds of thousands of members without compromising its core product philosophy. For Ethan, that growth supports a broader point: calm technology is not anti-technology, and designing for a user's attention instead of against it can sustain a product over time.

Author

The Attention Dividend and the Economics of Focus

Caldwell published The Attention Dividend in 2024. The book reframes focus as an economic advantage rather than a wellness trend, arguing that attention is not only worth protecting but worth investing. Directed deliberately into a small number of compounding pursuits, attention generates lasting returns. Scattered across feeds and alerts, it does not.

That framing moves digital wellbeing out of the self-care category and into a conversation about performance relevant to professionals, executives, and creators. A closer reading of the argument behind The Attention Dividend covers how the compounding model works. More on the book is collected on his personal website.

Design Training

How a BFA in Art and Design Shaped His View of Behavior

Caldwell's BFA training in art and design informed how he understood the relationship between form, environment, and behavior. Design disciplines treat the arrangement of a space or a surface as something that shapes what people notice, where they linger, and what they ignore. That lens carried directly into his later work on software.

The same lens explains his position on digital habits. Willpower is not the primary lever for changing them; environment design is. Redesigning the conditions of work, from notifications to interfaces to physical setup, produces more reliable results than discipline alone. The analysis of why willpower is the wrong tool sets out that reasoning in detail.
